# Code of conduct

lephycrypy+ wants its issues, pull requests and discussions to be a place where anyone can ask a
question, report a problem or suggest a change without being treated badly. This code of conduct
follows the spirit of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/).

## Expected

- Be respectful and patient, especially with people who are new to Rust, to cryptography or to
  open source.
- Discuss the work, not the person. Criticism of code, designs and documents is welcome when it
  is specific and constructive.
- Assume good intent, and ask before assuming someone meant harm.
- English and Turkish are both welcome in issues and pull requests.
- Respect people's privacy: do not post anyone's personal data, and never post real encrypted
  files, passwords, keys or paper-backup words, yours or anyone else's.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, personal attacks, or sexual, discriminatory or threatening language or
  images.
- Trolling or deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Publishing details of an unfixed security vulnerability in public (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)).
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in every space of the project on GitHub (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews,
commit messages) and when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

No e-mail address is published for reports. Use one of these:

- **For most cases:** use GitHub's own reporting. On the comment, issue or pull request, open the
  "…" menu and choose **Report content**. This goes to GitHub and to the maintainer, who can hide,
  edit or delete it and block the account.
- **For serious or private matters** (threats, harassment you do not want to describe in public,
  personal data that was exposed): open a private report through **Security › Report a
  vulnerability** on this repository and say at the top that it is a code-of-conduct report. Only
  the maintainer can see it.

Reports are handled privately; the person who reported is not named without their consent.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may, depending on how serious and how repeated the behaviour is:

1. ask for a correction, in public or in private;
2. hide, edit or delete comments, commits, issues and pull requests;
3. lock a discussion;
4. block the account from the project temporarily or permanently.

Decisions are explained to the person concerned where that is safe to do.
